# CANE CORSO BOOK BG — TRANSLATION RULES

## Translation objective

Produce Bulgarian that reads as naturally authored Bulgarian while preserving the meaning, structure, historical framing and speaker attribution of the Italian edition.

## Core principles

1. Translate meaning, not word order.
2. Do not invent facts.
3. Do not silently correct or strengthen historical claims.
4. Preserve distinctions between fact, hypothesis, interpretation and personal position.
5. Preserve the voice of Dott. Flavio Bruno separately from the editorial/narrative voice of Stefano De Tanini.
6. Do not remove material simply to make layout easier.
7. When Bulgarian is substantially longer, prefer concise natural reformulation before changing typography.
8. Do not translate directly inside artwork before the corresponding BG text is approved.

## Names and terminology

- Personal names remain names.
- Latin quotations/terms remain Latin unless the book itself supplies a translation.
- Historical breed/type names are governed by the terminology lock created in BG-02.
- `Cane Corso` may appear as `Кане Корсо` in Bulgarian prose according to the terminology lock.
- Do not alternate spellings without a documented reason.

## Titles and captions

- Translate headings, subheadings and captions consistently.
- Captions must match the corresponding image.
- Do not leave an Italian caption beneath a Bulgarian page.

## Tables / diagrams / infographics

- Translate labels using the same terminology as the surrounding body text.
- Preserve semantic hierarchy and relationships.
- Do not abbreviate terms merely to force them into cells without approval.

## Bibliography

- Preserve original bibliographic titles and publication data where normal bibliographic practice requires it.
- Translate only editorial section labels or explanatory text where appropriate.
- Never invent Bulgarian editions/titles.

## Quality gate

A translation is not approved if it is:
- literal but unnatural;
- semantically weaker/stronger than the source;
- inconsistent with terminology lock;
- ambiguous about who is speaking;
- shortened solely for layout without recorded editorial approval.
